From Domain Data to Agentic Experience

Bridging Data-Centric AI and Agent Learning
AAAI-27 Bridge Program
February 16–17, 2027
Palais des congrès de Montréal, Montréal, Canada

About the Bridge

AI agents pursue goals through observations, actions, tool use, and environmental feedback. Building and evaluating them requires data that capture meaningful interaction. Domain datasets, however, rarely specify the goals, constraints, tools, and success criteria needed to turn a professional workflow into a learning environment.

This bridge asks: how can domain knowledge, data, and workflows become grounded, informative, and responsibly governed experience for learning and evaluating agents?

We connect three communities that usually meet separately: data-centric AI and data engineering, agent learning and interactive systems, and domain science and practice. Data researchers contribute methods for collection, validation, coverage, governance, and provenance. Agent researchers contribute environment design, exploration, policy learning, and behavioral evaluation. Database and software-engineering researchers contribute methods for storing, querying, versioning, and tracing agent artifacts. Domain specialists define meaningful goals, professional constraints, and acceptable outcomes, including in experimental science, financial and service operations, physical engineering, and healthcare.

Participants will learn how to construct, curate, and manage agentic data through development and deployment, how to assess whether tasks and feedback are valid, and how to turn selected execution records into reusable learning and evaluation resources. The bridge is for researchers, graduate students, undergraduates, practitioners, and people who maintain data or environments.

The program is a two-day, in-person research meeting on February 16–17, 2027, at the Palais des congrès de Montréal, alongside AAAI-27. It combines ten invited talks, peer-reviewed contributed presentations, two poster sessions, and a moderated panel. The invited talks are the educational core: about five hours that introduce methods and open questions from complementary fields before turning to current research. Final room times follow AAAI’s allocation.



Topics

Five connected themes across the agentic-data lifecycle.

(1) Workflow representation

Which activities genuinely require sequential interaction? How should goals, observations, tools, hidden state, constraints, and human decision points be represented? What is lost when a professional workflow becomes a benchmark?

(2) Experience production and management

How should demonstrations, historical logs, simulations, and agent-generated trajectories be combined and validated? How can agent configurations, tool calls, memory updates, and feedback be linked across runs? Topics include execution consistency, behavioral coverage, trace schemas, versioning, data lineage, queryable storage, and access control, including when operational traces can become training examples or regression tests.

(3) Verification and evaluation

How should executable checks, expert review, human feedback, and learned evaluators be combined, given known biases in model-based judging? How can we detect evaluation shortcuts, account for delayed outcomes, and report uncertainty, while allowing domain-specific outcomes and constraints?

(4) Learning utility and transfer

How should difficulty be calibrated to the learner, the tools, and the execution budget? Which experience supports transferable planning or recovery skills? Discussion covers curricula, selection bias, training–test overlap, and controlled evaluation of transfer.

(5) Human and physical context

How should corrections, approvals, and collaborative actions be recorded, and how should human preference signals be incorporated? What changes when interaction involves sensors, robots, or laboratory instruments? Topics include temporal alignment, reset conditions, simulation fidelity, access rights, consent, and conditions for reuse.



Call for Papers

The AAAI-27 Bridge From Domain Data to Agentic Experience invites full papers, short papers, and extended abstracts on research results, datasets and benchmarks, experience-collection pipelines, reproducibility or failure analyses, and domain-grounded cases. Work in progress is welcome, including substantive domain cases that do not propose a new learning algorithm. Domain specialists are encouraged to contribute workflow case studies and posters that make assumptions and success criteria explicit.

Submissions will be reviewed for relevance, technical or empirical substance, clarity to a cross-disciplinary audience, and discussion value. Selected contributions will receive an oral presentation or a poster slot. We especially encourage junior and underrepresented researchers. Contributions are planned as non-archival. The bridge is in person; virtual presentations are not part of the AAAI-27 Bridge Program.
